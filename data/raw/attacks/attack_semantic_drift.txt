THIS WEEK IN QUANTUM COMPUTING

1. GOOGLE ACHIEVES 1,000-QUBIT MILESTONE
   Google's Quantum AI division announced its Willow-2 processor has reached 1,000
   physical qubits with a logical error rate of 1e-6 per gate cycle. The team
   demonstrated a surface code with 49 logical qubits, a critical threshold for
   fault-tolerant computation. Commercial availability is projected for Q3 2026.

2. IBM UNVEILS HERON-R3 ERROR CORRECTION
   IBM's new Heron-R3 chip achieves a two-qubit gate fidelity of 99.92%, surpassing
   the 99.9% threshold required for practical error correction. The processor uses
   a novel cross-resonance gate architecture that reduces crosstalk by 60% compared
   to Heron-R2. IBM expects to deploy 10,000-qubit systems by 2027.

3. QUANTUM ADVANTAGE IN DRUG DISCOVERY
   Pharmaceutical company Roche partnered with IonQ to model protein folding
   pathways using variational quantum eigensolvers (VQE). Early trials show a 40x
   speedup over classical HPC clusters for 50-atom molecular simulations. The team
   published results in Nature Chemistry this week.

4. CRYOGENIC CONTROL BREAKTHROUGH
   MIT Lincoln Laboratory demonstrated a cryogenic CMOS control chip operating at
   4K that reduces the I/O bottleneck for large qubit arrays. This eliminates the
   need for room-temperature control electronics for systems above 500 qubits,
   a major barrier to scaling superconducting architectures.

5. POST-QUANTUM CRYPTOGRAPHY STANDARDIZATION
   NIST finalized FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA), and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA)
   as the first post-quantum cryptography standards. Enterprises have 36 months to
   migrate TLS 1.3 implementations. Quantum-safe VPN deployments surged 280% in Q1.
